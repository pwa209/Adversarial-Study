# Full-population bridge and existing pulse-pair analysis

Frozen implementation scope, 24 October 2026. This is retrospective development using existing data. Success means a valid completed analysis, including null or adverse findings. No numerical direction, significance threshold or preferred biological family controls execution.

## Question and relation to the original proposal

The original proposal asks whether a clinical DoC-trained instrument can expose unexpected healthy neural episodes and whether competing biological accounts explain their dynamics and distinguishable perturbational predictions. The new analysis addresses a specific unresolved observation bottleneck: the native simulator saved nine population states, whereas the previous human bridge used cortical voltage alone. Can these full states predict human future neural dynamics beyond equally flexible cortical observations, and can configurations with similar cortical scores nevertheless differ in their system response to the same saved pulse?

The existing positive and null discovery, report and model-comparison findings remain unchanged. This extension does not by itself explain report discrepancies or establish a new positive discovery sample.

## Interpretation and outputs

A full-population advantage over matched delay and AR controls would identify useful hidden-state information within these effective native models. Distinct responses among score/spectrum-matched configurations would expose an ambiguity in cortical observation and generate falsifiable intervention predictions. Neither result identifies anatomical human populations: the simulator has nine effective populations at one spatial node, with delays and synaptic states. Its 9-by-9 coupling table is not the full dynamical Jacobian. Null advantages or unresolved response equivalence are retained and restrict the mechanism claim.

Aggregate outputs include qualified population cache/dispositions, training/validation freeze, validation grid, identical-case forecasts/losses, compatibility export, per-population and system responses, pair proofs, matched contrasts, descriptive summaries and source/input-bound receipts. New independent human replication and randomized interventions remain outside what these data can supply.
